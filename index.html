About

Where I work, and what I do there.

I'm a PhD candidate in Neuroscience at McGill University, working in Dr. Christopher Pack's lab at the Montreal Neurological Institute (The Neuro). My research sits at the intersection of systems neuroscience, computational neuroscience, and neuroengineering, with a focus on understanding how neural activity gives rise to visual perception and how we can interact with the brain through electrical stimulation and closed-loop systems.

My experimental work involves studying visual processing in awake macaques through behavioral experiments, electrophysiological recordings, and cortical microstimulation. I investigate how activity in visual areas such as V1, V4, and MT contributes to perception, with the broader goal of developing cortical visual prostheses. Alongside this, I build closed-loop experimental systems that integrate real-time neural recordings, computational algorithms, and electrical stimulation to study and influence neural activity.

On the computational side, I use deep neural networks, generative models, and neural data analysis to investigate visual representations and perceptual learning. My collaborations with researchers at Harvard University and Université de Montréal explore how artificial neural networks can help us understand biological vision and generate stimuli that probe neural processing.

Before my doctoral research, I worked on cardiac electrophysiology, developing a real-time interface between living cardiac tissue and GPU-based computational simulations. This experience shaped my broader interest in building systems where biological networks and artificial computation can interact dynamically.

Beyond research, I enjoy working across disciplines, bringing together experimental neuroscience, engineering, and computational approaches. I also contribute to neuroscience education through teaching at McGill and mentoring students at Neuromatch Academy.

Research

Three projects, one pattern: tissue and computer exchange signals fast enough to change each other.

A closed loop between heart cells and a simulation

Cultured cardiac monolayers and a GPU model of cardiac tissue respond to each other in real time, with light as the link. The same architecture could train neural tissue.

Published in PLOS Computational Biology, 2026

Hierarchical control of visual perceptual learning with model-synthesized stimuli

A model of the primate dorsal stream generates motion videos that differ at one chosen stage of the hierarchy. The page shows every stimulus.

Toward a V4 visual prosthesis

Electrical stimulation of shape-selective domains in macaque V4, and neurons that choose the images they see.
